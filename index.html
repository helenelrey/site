Archaeologist and cultural studies scholar working on how communities in medieval Central and Inner Asia negotiated overlapping identities. Particularly interested in how these negotiations were translated into the material record, and how digital and spatial methods can help make sense of scattered legacy evidence.

Current research

Materialising Entangled Identities: A Digital Archaeological Study of East Syriac Christianity in 13th–14th Century Turco-Mongol Central Eurasia

This project explores how the spatial organisation and material culture of East Syriac Christian practice among Turco-Mongol communities reflect the negotiation of overlapping identities — and how digital methods can help reconstruct these processes from fragmentary, dispersed evidence.

The case study areas are the Semirechye/Zhetisu region of southeastern Kazakhstan and northern Kyrgyzstan, and central Inner and Outer Mongolia. Methods include spatial and network analysis, supported by a CIDOC CRM-structured PostgreSQL/PostGIS database seeking to bring dispersed legacy data into a single analysable form. The analysis draws on practice-centred and entanglement approaches to material culture, alongside a critical reading of Silk Roads scholarship and its inherited categories.
